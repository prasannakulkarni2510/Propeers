# Cold DM: Professional Outreach Playbook

## What a cold DM is for

A direct message is best for opening a conversation—not for sending a cover letter in miniature. A recipient should be able to read it on a phone and understand the point immediately. Keep the first message compact, typically **40–90 words**, and check the platform’s current connection-note or message limits before sending.

## The DM formula

**Context + credible fit + small ask.**

- **Context:** Why this person, specifically?
- **Credible fit:** One matching skill or piece of work, not a full technology list.
- **Small ask:** A short question, brief conversation, or permission to share a project.

Do not start with “Hi, I need a job,” “Please refer me,” or a long personal story. They may be honest sentiments, but they give the recipient no useful basis for judging fit.

## Adapt to who you are messaging

| Recipient | Lead with | Ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer / practitioner | A technical post, project, or problem they discussed | One focused question or a short conversation |
| Hiring manager | The role’s requirement and relevant evidence | Whether the fit is worth exploring |
| Recruiter | Exact role, location/work eligibility if relevant, strongest match | Process or requirement clarification |
| Founder | Specific product observation and a useful capability | Whether the problem is currently relevant |
| Alumni / shared community | Genuine shared context, then concise fit | Advice or a pointer—not an automatic referral |
| Researcher | A paper/result and a narrow follow-up question | Guidance on fit or relevant reading |

## Templates

### 1. Connection request to an engineer

Hi [Name] — I saw your post/project on [specific topic]. I’m working in [relevant area] and have been building with [one or two matching skills]. I’d like to connect and learn more about how your team approaches [specific problem].

### 2. To a hiring manager after applying

Hi [Name], I applied for [exact role]. My closest matches are [requirement] through [evidence] and [requirement] through [evidence]. I was especially interested in [specific team/product detail]. I’ve shared the role-specific resume here: [link, if appropriate]. Is there anything you recommend I highlight for the team?

Do not attach or link a resume if platform policy or the person’s preference says not to. Avoid making them repeat the formal application process.

### 3. To a recruiter

Hi [Name] — I’m exploring [exact role family] opportunities and noticed [role title / requisition] at [Company]. My relevant experience is in [two requirements, backed by evidence]. Is the position still active, and are there must-have skills I should confirm before applying?

### 4. To a founder at an early-stage company

Hi [Name] — I noticed [specific launch/feature/post]. I’ve built [short example] using [relevant skills] and was curious whether [specific problem] is something the team is working on. If useful, here’s a brief demo: [link]. Open to a quick exchange if it is relevant.

### 5. To a researcher / professor

Hello Dr. [Surname] — I read your work on [paper/topic], particularly [specific detail]. I have experience with [relevant methods/project] and am trying to understand [focused question]. Is there a paper or direction you would recommend I study next? Thank you.

### 6. To an alumnus or mutual connection

Hi [Name] — I noticed we both [real shared context]. I’m targeting [role type] and have been building experience in [two relevant areas]. Your path into [team/industry] caught my attention. Would you be open to sharing one piece of advice about what matters most when applying to teams like yours?

### 7. After a person accepts the connection

Thanks for connecting, [Name]. I’m exploring [specific role area] and noticed [specific detail about their work]. One thing I’m trying to learn is [one meaningful question]. No worries if you’re busy—your perspective would be helpful.

### 8. A polite final follow-up

Hi [Name] — bringing this up once more in case it got buried. I was asking about [role/topic] because [one-line fit]. If now isn’t a good time or you’re not the right contact, no worries. Thanks either way.

## Different fields need different proof

- **AI / GenAI:** Mention what the system does, what you implemented, and how you checked output quality. “Built an LLM app” is too broad.
- **Backend / platform:** Mention an API, service, data pipeline, or reliability task. Highlight testing and error handling when true.
- **Data / analytics:** Mention the data problem and how the output supported a decision or workflow.
- **Product engineering:** Show that you can connect user needs to implementation and trade-offs.
- **Research:** Ask about one technical idea or method; do not simply ask for “any opportunity.”
- **AEC / engineering software:** Connect domain understanding with software or AI implementation if the role values both.

## Message sequencing

1. **Before connecting:** Send a short, genuine note if the platform allows it.
2. **After acceptance:** Start with context and one question; do not immediately send a multi-paragraph pitch.
3. **If there is no response:** Wait several business days before one follow-up.
4. **If there is still no response:** Stop. Do not message across every platform or contact the person’s colleagues to force an answer.
5. **If referred elsewhere:** Thank the person and mention the introduction accurately.

## The credibility test

A DM should never imply that you know the person, have a referral, read their work, or built something you have not actually seen or built. Personalization is not inserting a name; it is identifying a real reason to speak with that person.

## Common mistakes

- Asking for a referral before establishing relevance.
- Sending a resume with no explanation of the role or fit.
- Listing 12 technologies with no proof.
- Copy-pasting the same message to people at the same company.
- Demanding a reply, interview, or call.
- Overexplaining unemployment, financial pressure, or personal circumstances in the opening message.
- Treating a lack of response as permission to escalate contact.

## Checklist

- [ ] Under the platform’s limit and readable on mobile.
- [ ] Personalization is factual and specific.
- [ ] One or two skills match the recipient’s work.
- [ ] One small ask.
- [ ] No false familiarity or unsupported claims.
