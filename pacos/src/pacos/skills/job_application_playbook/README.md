# Job Application Writing Playbook

A hiring-manager-oriented guide to writing targeted outreach and application documents. The goal is not to sound impressive in the abstract; it is to make the reader quickly understand **why this candidate, for this role, at this company, now**.

## Files

1. [`cold-email.md`](cold-email.md) — cold emails to hiring managers, founders, engineers, recruiters, and research leads.
2. [`cold-dm.md`](cold-dm.md) — concise LinkedIn and other professional direct messages.
3. [`cv-tips.md`](cv-tips.md) — CV strategy, especially for academic, research, and international applications.
4. [`resume-tips.md`](resume-tips.md) — role-targeted, ATS-readable resumes for industry hiring.
5. [`cover-letter.md`](cover-letter.md) — cover letters built around evidence and the employer’s problem.

## The core decision rule

Before writing, answer these five questions:

1. **What kind of organization is this?** Startup, large product company, services/consulting firm, research lab, engineering/AEC company, or public/nonprofit organization?
2. **What kind of role is this?** AI/ML, applied GenAI, backend/platform, data engineering/analytics, research, or a hybrid product-engineering role?
3. **What does the role need proved?** Shipping, reliability, experimentation, data quality, customer impact, research depth, or domain knowledge?
4. **Which two or three candidate facts prove that fit?** Select evidence, not a list of every tool the candidate has touched.
5. **What is the smallest reasonable next step?** A brief conversation, a referral, feedback on fit, or permission to send a project/resume.

## Candidate profile: evidence to draw from

Use only facts that are accurate and can be explained in an interview. For a candidate with a background like the profile used to shape this playbook, potentially relevant evidence includes:

- Python and SQL for data processing, pipelines, and engineering tasks.
- GenAI/LLM work, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), embeddings, vector databases, and evaluation concepts.
- FastAPI/backend development, LangGraph or agent workflows, and React when relevant to an end-to-end application.
- Internal dashboards or workflow tooling that helped people test or operate systems.
- Experience applying AI and geometry-related problem solving in the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) domain.
- Mechanical engineering and quantitative coursework as supporting context for technical reasoning—not as a substitute for software evidence.

**Do not claim** production scale, revenue impact, model accuracy improvements, cloud expertise, ownership, or user counts unless there is evidence. Replace placeholders such as `[measured result]` only with a defensible fact. A well-explained small project is stronger than an inflated claim.

## A practical workflow

1. Read the job description and identify three repeated requirements.
2. Inspect the company’s product, customers, engineering blog, job page, or public repositories.
3. Choose the matching document and template.
4. Add one specific company/role observation and two pieces of proof.
5. Remove every sentence that could be sent unchanged to ten unrelated employers.
6. Check names, company, role title, links, dates, spelling, and factual accuracy.
7. Track the outreach date and follow-up. Stop after a clear rejection or opt-out.

## Quick quality test

A busy hiring manager should be able to answer within 15 seconds:

- What role or problem is this person targeting?
- What relevant evidence do they have?
- Why are they contacting *this* company or person?
- What exactly are they asking me to do?

If any answer is unclear, revise before sending.
